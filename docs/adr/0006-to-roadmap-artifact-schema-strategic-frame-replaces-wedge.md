# to-roadmap's schema replaces "Wedge" with a narrowly-scoped Strategic Frame field, and gives each Move a two-part Evidence Threshold

The wedge/strategic-bridge research (`roadmap-strategy-bridge-mechanics.md`, Q5) found a real tension: every primary source's own worked "wedge" example describes expansion into a different product or market than the starting point — a claim a `to-roadmap` session, scoped to read one pitch and never re-fetch the vision, cannot substantiate. The research also found Cagan/SVPG, the source grounding the rest of this skill's strategy vocabulary, never uses "wedge" at all, and April Dunford's own usage means something narrower than market expansion. This ticket picks the narrow reading — later Moves stay within the one approved pitch's own Problem/Solution bounds — and renames the field to **Strategic Frame**, folding problem-space-claimed, constraints, and differentiation into one structured field rather than three siblings. This avoids both a field name that promises more than the skill's single-pitch scope can support, and a proliferation of top-level strategy fields with no evidence they vary independently at this scale.

The Moves list keeps four fields — Description, Appetite (reusing to-pitch's two-tier timebox), Evidence Threshold, Status — with four Move states (pending/active/proven/invalidated) matching Shape Up's one-bet-at-a-time cadence. Evidence Threshold is deliberately lighter than to-pitch's four-part Riskiest Assumption (claim/threshold/test/timebox): since `to-roadmap` only states the threshold and `to-milestone` executes the gate check later (an already-decided split), the field needs just **signal** (the observable result) and **threshold** (the bar), not a test plan or timebox.

Considered and rejected: keeping the "Wedge" name with a narrowed definition (rejected — the name itself carries the cross-market connotation regardless of how the field is defined, inviting future misuse); the literal cross-market reading, flagged as aspirational (rejected — asserts something the skill has no evidence for); splitting Strategic Frame into separate Wedge/Differentiation/Constraints fields (rejected — no source treats these as independently varying at a single-pitch scale).

Caps mirror to-pitch's pattern: Strategic Frame ~150 words, each Move's Description ~50 words, Moves list capped at 6 open (non-invalidated) Moves at a time, soft total budget ~400–500 words (non-enforced).
