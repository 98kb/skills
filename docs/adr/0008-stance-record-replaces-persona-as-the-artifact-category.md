# The founder-stance artifact is a stance record, not a persona, and the skill is `to-stance`

The artifact-category research (`stance-artifact-category.md`) scored the design persona **1 of 6** against the constraints the three sibling passes established, failing on the object, the boundary mechanism, the lifecycle *and* the loading shape — not on execution. NN/g's own definition settles the object question: a persona is "a fictional, yet realistic, description of a typical or target user," a design target composed from many people, where this artifact records one real named person as a historical record. Two further findings make the mismatch load-bearing rather than cosmetic. Chapman & Milham argue the fictional layer is precisely what makes such an artifact unfalsifiable, so persona components are not inert padding — they immunise the record against correction. And the boundary research found design personas carry **no boundary mechanism at all**, an empty result in the territory the originating issue expected to be richest.

Archetype, thinking styles, jobs-to-be-done and competency models are eliminated on their own definitions: every one has a **non-individual object**. Archetype in particular is ruled out by NN/g themselves, who demonstrate the persona's invented layer is losslessly deletable because personas and archetypes "represent the same data."

No established category fits. Five properties are required — object = one named person; unit and index = a question with positions attached; lifecycle = frozen, superseded by dated append; standing = persuasive never binding, with the recorder's inferences attributed separately; access = a small index over retrievable entries — and they are owned by five different categories, none holding more than four. This is recorded as a genuine gap rather than forced into the nearest fit. The nearest neighbour to **build on** is the IBIS issue record, the only category owning question-indexed positions *and* never-an-authority together, the latter enforced structurally (no stopping rule, no way to mark an issue resolved) rather than by disclaimer. The nearest neighbour on **purpose** is the advance health-care instruction, which is to be studied and not adopted: Fagerlin & Schneider measured that genre failing at scale, and their finding is carried forward as evidence against building the artifact at all, not as a design note.

The name follows the unit, and the unit was arrived at independently three times. The loadable-context research named the retrievable unit a "position file" from agent-memory and retrieval sources with no exposure to design rationale; gIBIS defines "A Position is a statement or assertion which resolves the Issue"; and the originating issue's own prior-art notes used the noun twice unprompted. Three disjoint literatures already agreed on the unit — only the container was misnamed. Concretely: the skill is `to-stance`, the artifact is a **stance record**, and its entries are **positions**, each keyed to the **question** it was elicited about.

Considered and rejected: *persona* and *archetype* (impersonation sections arrive by gravity, precisely because they are the most trivially elicitable material); *profile* (implies psychometrics and a completeness claim nothing supports); *principles* or *credo* (prescriptive and authoritative by genre — the standing is exactly wrong, and the research flags this as the most dangerous near-miss because the shape otherwise fits); *founder decision record* or any ADR echo (implies bindingness, and collides with this repo's own `docs/adr/`); *charter*, *doctrine*, *playbook* (all imperative).

The cost of naming a gap is accepted deliberately: "stance record" is not an established genre, so it inherits no machinery **and** no reader expectations. Every convention — the question key, the basis field, the open-questions register, the supersession marker — must be stated by the skill and checked by the eval, because no reader will supply them by habit. This ADR settles the category and the name only. Whether the artifact is worth building remains open, with the strongest evidence currently against it.
