# `to-stance` mechanisms: computed over declared, and approval collapses into authorship

Six mechanism questions were split off the go/no-go so they could not dilute it, deliberately blocked until the go was called. With the go in ([ADR 0011](0011-conditional-go-the-stance-record-is-the-delta-between-stated-and-evidenced.md)) they resolve, and they resolve with a single shape: **wherever the design was about to declare a field, the field is computed from something already in the record instead.** That is not a stylistic preference — it is forced by freezing. A declared field on a frozen record is either mutable, and breaks the freeze, or immutable, and goes stale. Four of the six answers below are instances of it.

## Retrieval, ratified — and it was already load-bearing

Nobody loads a stance record as a unit. The always-loaded index plus per-question position retrieval is **ratified**, but the honest framing is that it stopped being an open question when ADR 0011 was written: "recorded but never indexed" quarantines the residue only if retrieval runs through the index. Rejecting the two-tier shape would have unmade the mechanism that carried the go.

Ratified with its cost stated. With no gate (nothing downstream may gate on this artifact) and no pipeline hop, "always-loaded" is a claim about wiring in someone else's repo. That is **Fagerlin & Schneider's unmitigated first condition wearing a second hat** — the record failing not because the founder never returns to write it, but because nobody wired it to be read. *Who* wires it remains Phase 2 composition; that this is the artifact's second single point of failure is recorded here.

The cross-check owed between the two research passes resolves **half in favour**. Their convergence is real and now doubly confirmed: the absence of an index line *is* the computable no-match, reached independently from the retrieval side and the archival side. But the claim that per-question retrieval is *better* for never-an-authority because "a deliberate lookup is visible and attributable" **does not survive**. Nothing in the design persists a retrieval; a lookup is visible in a transcript nobody keeps. This is the same crack ADR 0011 refused to inherit when it declined "no disclaimers anywhere" — a human-reader finding transposed to an agent reader without evidence. **The never-an-authority work is done by the computable no-match and the residue quarantine, and none of it by lookups being observable.**

## Approval is not a lifecycle state here; it is the authorship field

The [approval & completion gate pattern](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/9) decided on the sibling milestone map specifies an embedded marker (approver + timestamp), a **blocking** downstream gate, and a marker that **clears back to "drafted" on in-place revision**. Against `to-stance`, the gate is refused outright by the founding constraint that nothing downstream gates on this artifact, and the clear-on-revision clause has no subject in a frozen record. The expected answer was therefore "keep the marker, drop the gate."

**That is wrong, and for a reason the tension's usual framing hides.** A marker exists to distinguish agent-drafted from human-blessed. [ADR 0010](0010-stance-record-accretes-by-queued-sitting.md) already holds that **no agent ever writes the founder-said layer** — so for a position there is no drafted state to leave, and the marker would record a transition that cannot occur. Approval keeps a subject in exactly the two places an agent wrote something: derived entries and agent-appended register entries. In both, what is wanted is *who authored this*, which the schema already carries.

**`to-stance` therefore inherits neither the gate nor the marker.** The drafted/approved binary collapses into ADR 0010's authorship rule. This is recorded as a `to-stance`-specific divergence and explicitly **not** as a proposed amendment to the milestone map's pattern — that map has its four skills in view and this one does not. One clause does survive with a subject: the **index** is revisable description, so clear-on-revision applies there, and it is handed to the index-revision ticket rather than settled here.

## One register, three origins, carried by a field

The register-versus-backlog split was framed as record against workflow state. Two things moved underneath it. ADR 0011 added the date-provenance field that already carries the evidential difference; and ADR 0011 added a **third** origin the two-column framing predates — an entry reopened from a position's **residue**.

**One register, three origins, distinguished by an `origin` field: `asked`, `residue`, `observed`.** Splitting into two stores would break ADR 0011's claim that the register is a *product of* position-writing, since residue-reopened and agent-appended entries would land in different places while feeding the same next sitting. And the "backlog wants clearing" argument inverts once ADR 0011 is applied: an agent-appended no-match is a **contemporaneous episode capture**, the most evidentially valuable thing the register holds. Filing the artifact's best evidence in a to-do list is the wrong way round. The register stays file-based; the tracker-backed issue mode is not imported, and neither is the mutable workflow state that comes with it.

The values are named for the means, not the occasion. Not "seed-opened": an *accrual* sitting also raises questions that go nowhere, so a name keyed to the seed would be false by the second sitting. `asked` states that the subject was asked and it went nowhere, and stays true forever. `observed` is the only origin yielding a contemporaneous date — so **`origin` predicts date provenance rather than duplicating it**, and the two fields stay non-redundant.

## No status vocabulary: status is computed from dates and appends

Nothing had settled how a register entry says it is open, and an illustrative sample had already improvised one. **No status vocabulary is adopted.** An entry carries an entry date; closure is a dated append naming the position that closed it; and **"open" is the absence of a closure append** — computed, not asserted, with no label to flip on a frozen record. Mutable labels on a frozen record is the category error the boundary research warns against, and this repo's five triage labels run the opposite lifecycle besides: `needs-triage` exists to be cleared, whereas an open register entry staying open is legitimate and informative.

The improvised sample's nuances are not lost, they are reclassified as **history**: "asked twice, deflected" is two dated appends, "declined" is a dated append recording a refusal. None is a status, and the entry stays legitimately open underneath them — which is what POMR means in treating an open problem as a fact about the record.

## No fitness declaration and no currency marker, and both refusals are load-bearing

**Fitness is derived, not declared.** The set of **conflict classes** present in the index *is* the statement of what class of decision the record is competent for. A declared statement fails three ways at once: it goes stale the moment a sitting adds a class, it has no evidential basis because nobody has measured what class this record is good for, and being prose it is exactly what the boundary research says gets re-read in light of the reader's own preferences — the failure mode it would have been added to prevent. To make the derivation actually free, **conflict class goes on the index line**; otherwise the classes are not visible in what is always loaded and "derived" would quietly mean "stored twice." The cost is accepted explicitly: a class list aids discovery, it does not *refuse* the reader who wants a position to exist. That refusal is per-entry evidenced scope plus the residue quarantine, and nothing stronger is available, because anything stronger is a gate.

**No record-level expiry.** The sunset device is cheap and its source applies it to itself, but currency bites asymmetrically here and the design already splits along that seam. An **episode** is retrospective and dated — a 2024 occasion is as true in 2030, and its age is visible per-entry for free. What goes stale is the stated, present-tense disposition, which is the **residue** — already quarantined from retrieval and already queued into the register. **The stale-able part is the part that cannot be retrieved.** Beyond that, an expiry on a deliberately frozen append-only archival record is incoherent, and ADR 0010's stored next-sitting date is already the come-back device; a sunset would be a second one with worse semantics. What is adopted instead is ISAD(G)'s own device: the index carries a **description date**, so the always-loaded tier shows its own age.

## The budget is a tripwire, on the wrong meter as originally stated

The 200-line / 25KB index budget is vendor-convergent practice on two converging first-party limits, and the research that produced it is explicit that the failure mode it was sent to find has never been measured. The prototype settles the substance: the routing-line axis is roughly **25 years** away at a realistic sitting cadence, and the pressure that actually bites is **bytes**, arriving from a component that is not routing lines at all.

**Adopted as a tripwire on total always-loaded bytes, with its weak provenance stated in the same breath; the 200-line meter is dropped** because lines measure the thing that does not grow. Nothing in the design rests on the number's precision, which is precisely why adopting it costs nothing. One condition: a tripwire whose trip action is unspecified is decoration, and the only sheddable mass in the always-loaded tier is what is not routing lines — which is the open ticket on the register's relation to that tier, not this decision. **The number is adopted here; the trip action is that ticket's**, and this is a second and independent reason it has to run.
