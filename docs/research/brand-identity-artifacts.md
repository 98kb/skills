# What set of written artifacts do published brand-identity practitioners actually produce, and what is in each one?

Research for [#137](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/137), under [Map: to-brand-identity — specify the skill completely, so the build map decides nothing (#136)](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/136). The hypothesis under test is the one the effort was proposed with: that the output is a `brand-canon` plus a `brand-guidelines`, and that those two names carve the field at a strategy/execution joint. The map's sourcing bar is stricter than the sibling docs' because the literature is more polluted: **a structural claim needs two independent named practitioners with published, citable method**, primary sources preferred — the practitioner's own published text, first-party method documentation, or a published standard. Agency SEO content is inadmissible and none is cited below; Sinek is not cited at all, because no claim in this doc needed him. Where a source's primary text could not be retrieved it is named and not scored rather than scored off a secondary summary, and every section below states which of its claims clear the two-source bar and which do not.

## Q1 — What artifacts does the literature actually name, and do the names converge?

**Verdict: a durable written strategy artifact is universal and clears the bar many times over. Its *name* does not converge at all — six serious sources give it six different names, and "canon" is not one of them. The execution-layer artifact is the opposite case: the name converges hard on "guidelines" / "standards", and it is the one word the field genuinely shares.**

- **The strategy artifact, by six names.** Wheeler calls it the **brand brief** and is explicit that it is a document: "a simple one-page diagram" ([Logo Geek interview](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/design-a-brand-identity-with-alina-wheeler/)). Aaker calls it the **brand vision** — "an articulated description of the aspirational image for the brand: what you want the brand to stand for in the eyes of customers" ([The First Step in Building a Brand](https://prophet.com/2014/10/the-first-step-in-building-a-brand/)). Neumeier calls it the **brand commitment matrix**: "The matrix is a simple tool for crafting and organizing the six key elements of a brand" ([Peachpit](https://www.peachpit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2437847)). Keller calls it **brand positioning**, with four named components ([*Brand Planning*](https://www.marksherrington.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/ebook-brand-planning.pdf), ch. 1). Collins and Porras call it **core ideology** ([Vision Framework](https://www.jimcollins.com/tools/vision-framework.pdf)). Meyerson records **brand pillars** as a live term of art — "important strengths or aspects of a brand that support or add dimension to the brand's core idea(s)" ([Definition of brand pillars](https://howbrandsarebuilt.com/definition-what-are-brand-pillars/)).
- **That the artifact exists and is written down clears the bar overwhelmingly** — Wheeler, Aaker, Neumeier, Keller and Collins/Porras are five independent named practitioners with published method, and all five produce a short written strategy document. **That any particular *name* is the field's name does not clear the bar at all.** No two of the five agree.
- **"Canon" appears in none of them.** It was searched for and not found in any primary source consulted. This is a null result rather than a refutation, but it is the same null result [ADR 0006](https://github.com/98kb/skills/blob/main/docs/adr/0006-to-roadmap-artifact-schema-strategic-frame-replaces-wedge.md) and [ADR 0008](https://github.com/98kb/skills/blob/main/docs/adr/0008-stance-record-replaces-persona-as-the-artifact-category.md) were written about: a name chosen before the field was read, carrying connotations — fixity, completeness, scriptural authority — that no source in this literature attaches to this artifact, and that Aaker explicitly denies of its downstream half (Q2).
- **The execution artifact, by two names that are the same name.** Wheeler names it in the ordinary way — phase five, "managing assets", is "when the revitalised brand is launched to the world and **the guidelines** are made available" ([Logo Geek interview](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/design-a-brand-identity-with-alina-wheeler/)). Aaker's seventh step is to "create a communication guide using brand position" ([The First Step in Building a Brand](https://prophet.com/2014/10/the-first-step-in-building-a-brand/)). Every published standard consulted uses the same family of words in its own title: Wikimedia's [Visual identity guidelines](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Visual_identity_guidelines), NASA's [Graphics Standards Manual](https://www.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/atoms/files/nasa_graphics_manual_nhb_1430-2_jan_1976.pdf), Mailchimp's [Content Style Guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/), GDS's [Writing to GOV.UK standards](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/). **This clears the bar**: two independent named practitioners plus four independent published standards.
- **A third artifact nobody put in the hypothesis: the project brief.** Wheeler names it and separates it by hand — see Q5. Johnson's structure names a whole half-step for it: his contents run Investigate / Strategy and Narrative / **2.5 Bridging the Gap** / Design / Implement / Engage or Revive ([Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/products/branding-in-five-and-a-half-steps)).

## Q2 — Does any source distinguish a strategy-layer artifact from an execution-layer one?

**Verdict: yes, emphatically, and this is the best-corroborated finding in the pass. Four independent practitioners draw the line, and two of them make drawing it the point of their whole method. The hypothesis's *split* is right. It is the hypothesis's *count* that is wrong — the sources draw two lines, not one, producing three layers.**

- **Neumeier makes the split the thesis of the book.** His own site describes *The Brand Gap* as "the first unified theory of branding—a set of five disciplines that let companies bridge the gap **between brand strategy and brand execution**" ([martyneumeier.com](https://www.martyneumeier.com/the-brand-gap)). The gap *is* the strategy/execution seam; the book exists because organisations have one and not the other.
- **Aaker draws the line as a subset relation, and names what crosses it.** Brand position is "that part of the brand identity that is to be actively communicated"; "the current positioning often emphasizes the brand vision elements that are credible and deliverable" ([It Starts With a Brand Vision](https://prophet.com/2014/03/185-it-starts-with-a-brand-vision/)). His seven-step sequence ends at step seven with "create a communication guide using brand position" ([The First Step in Building a Brand](https://prophet.com/2014/10/the-first-step-in-building-a-brand/)) — the execution artifact is generated *from* a filtered subset of the strategy artifact, not authored alongside it.
- **Aaker also gives the sharpest audience test for which side of the line a claim is on.** The brand essence is "an internally communicated concept", whereas "the centerpiece of the position often is a tagline communicated externally, that need not and usually does not correspond to the brand essence" ([It Starts With a Brand Vision](https://prophet.com/2014/03/185-it-starts-with-a-brand-vision/)). Internal-facing versus external-facing is a *harder* discriminator than strategy-versus-execution, because it is checkable: you can ask of any line, would this ever be shown to a customer?
- **Keller draws the same line inside a single component.** The brand mantra's "purpose is to ensure that all employees within the organization and all external marketing partners understand what the brand most fundamentally is to represent with consumers so that they can adjust their actions accordingly" ([*Brand Planning*](https://www.marksherrington.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/ebook-brand-planning.pdf)). It is a governance device aimed at the people who execute, not at customers — and he describes its function in exactly the terms an execution artifact would be described in: "brand mantras are designed to create a mental filter to screen out brand-inappropriate marketing activities or actions of any type".
- **Wheeler draws it by refusing to let two documents merge** — see Q5, where the refusal is stated in her own words.
- **Johnson institutionalises the seam as a numbered step.** The half-step, "2.5 Bridging the Gap", sits between "Strategy and Narrative" and "Design" ([Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/products/branding-in-five-and-a-half-steps)). A methodology that has to invent a fractional step to hold the translation is evidence that the two things either side of it are genuinely different artifacts.
- **Clears the bar: yes, four ways over** (Neumeier, Aaker, Keller, Wheeler, with Johnson corroborating on structure). **Does not clear the bar: the claim that the split is two-way.** Aaker's own sequence is three-part — vision (durable, internal), position (revisable, the communicated subset), communication guide/programs (execution) — and Wheeler's is three-part too, with the creative brief as a third, disposable document. The two-artifact hypothesis compresses a three-layer structure, and it compresses it at the *wrong* joint: it fuses the revisable communicated position into whichever of its two artifacts you happen to put positioning in.

## Q3 — What is actually in the strategy-layer artifact, field by field?

**Verdict: seven fields clear the two-source bar. Three do not, and one of the three — points-of-parity — is the most interesting thing any single source contributes.**

The full field lists, as each source states them:

- **Wheeler's brand brief**, from her own enumeration ([Logo Geek interview](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/design-a-brand-identity-with-alina-wheeler/)): core purpose ("why do they exist right beyond just making money?"), audience ("who are their customers and what do they need?"), personality attributes ("whether they want to be shallow and edgy or buttoned up"), the top three or four key products or services, proof points ("why they will be successful"), values ("what are the values that hold this organisation together?"), and value proposition ("What are the benefits? Are they functional, or are they social or are they emotional?"). One page.
- **Neumeier's brand commitment matrix**, six elements in two columns ([Peachpit](https://www.peachpit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2437847)): customer side — **Identity**, **Aims**, tribe **mores**; company side — **Purpose**, **Onlyness**, **Values**. The elements are not independent: "The customer's identity has to match up with the company's purpose. The customer's aims have to match up with the offering's 'onlyness.' And the tribe's 'mores' or rules have to match up with the company's values."
- **Aaker's brand vision** ([It Starts With a Brand Vision](https://prophet.com/2014/03/185-it-starts-with-a-brand-vision/)): "six to 12 vision elements", "prioritized into the two to five that are the most compelling and differentiating, termed the 'core vision elements,' while the others are labeled 'extended vision elements.'" Extended elements "add texture to the brand vision" and provide "a home for important aspects of the brand, such as a brand personality." Plus an optional brand essence — "there are times in which it actually gets in the way and is better omitted."
- **Keller's positioning**, four components ([*Brand Planning*](https://www.marksherrington.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/ebook-brand-planning.pdf)): "1. a competitive frame of reference in terms of the target market and nature of competition 2. the points-of-difference in terms of strong, favorable, and unique brand associations 3. the points-of-parity in terms of brand associations that negate any existing or potential points-of-difference by competitors 4. a brand mantra that summarizes the essence of the brand and key points-of-difference in 3-5 words."
- **Collins and Porras's core ideology** ([Vision Framework](https://www.jimcollins.com/tools/vision-framework.pdf)): core values (three to five) and core purpose, with the envisioned future — goal plus vivid description — kept deliberately separate from them.

**Clears the two-source bar:**

- **Purpose** — Wheeler ("core purpose"), Neumeier ("Purpose"), Collins/Porras ("core purpose"). Three sources.
- **Values** — Wheeler, Neumeier, Collins/Porras. Three sources, and Collins/Porras alone supply a test for them (Q6).
- **Personality** — Wheeler ("personality attributes"), Aaker (extended elements are explicitly "a home for important aspects of the brand, such as a brand personality"). Two sources, and note both put personality in the *secondary* tier rather than the core.
- **Audience / customer** — Wheeler ("audience"), Neumeier (the entire left-hand column: Identity, Aims, mores), Keller ("competitive frame of reference in terms of the target market"). Three sources.
- **Differentiator** — Neumeier ("Onlyness"), Keller ("points-of-difference"), Aaker (core elements are the ones "most compelling and differentiating"). Three sources, and the strongest convergence of any field in this pass: every serious source has a field whose entire job is *what is true of us and not of them*.
- **Proof / credibility** — Wheeler ("proof points"), Aaker (the position emphasises the elements that are "credible and deliverable"), Keller (a POD must be believed — "Consumers must be convinced"). Three sources.
- **A short essence line, and it is optional** — Aaker (brand essence, "optional", internally communicated) and Keller (brand mantra, "three- to five-word phrases"). Two sources, and both are careful about it: Aaker says it sometimes "gets in the way and is better omitted"; Keller's is explicitly internal, not a tagline.

**Does not clear the two-source bar:**

- **Points-of-parity** — Keller only. This is the field most worth flagging anyway, because it is the one field in the whole literature that admits *what you must claim in order not to be disqualified*, as distinct from what makes you different: associations "that negate any existing or potential points-of-difference by competitors". Single-sourced, so it cannot land as structure; recorded because a brand artifact with only differentiators in it has no place to put table stakes.
- **Value proposition as a named field with functional/social/emotional sub-types** — Wheeler only, in that phrasing.
- **Key products and services** — Wheeler only.
- **Narrative / brand story as a field** — Johnson's step is named "Strategy and Narrative" ([Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/products/branding-in-five-and-a-half-steps)), but this is a publisher's contents listing, not the author's text, and no second source names narrative as a field of the strategy artifact. Does not clear.
- **Messaging templates, message houses, boilerplate** — **no primary source found at all.** This is a genuine gap, and it matters: the hypothesis's `brand-guidelines` was described as holding "messaging templates", and nothing in the retrievable published method supports messaging templates as an artifact or a section. Searches for it returned agency content exclusively, which the bar excludes.

## Q4 — What is in the execution-layer artifact?

**Verdict: the published standards agree on a shape, and the shape is bad news for the hypothesis — the real-world `brand-guidelines` document is dominated by visual rules and is addressed to people outside the founding team, which is precisely the scope the map has ruled out. The verbal half exists and is well-specified, but it lives in a *different* published document with a different name.**

- **The visual guidelines document.** Wikimedia's is representative and fully retrievable; its sections are: why guidelines matter, the identity and its elements, using the identity, protecting the marks, placement of legal attributes, minimum sizes, clear space, colour values, **"Do not…"**, 3D model use, and co-branding ([Visual identity guidelines](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Visual_identity_guidelines)). NASA's 1976 manual is the genre's origin point and is published by the agency itself ([NHB 1430-2](https://www.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/atoms/files/nasa_graphics_manual_nhb_1430-2_jan_1976.pdf)); its contents could not be extracted, so nothing is claimed from it beyond the genre's existence and its status as an agency handbook.
- **The reader of a visual guidelines document is frequently a stranger.** Wikimedia's scope statement is explicit: "We created these guidelines to help authorized re-users of the Wikimedia Foundation's official marks, including Wikipedia, represent our identity in a way that is consistent with the quality, impact, and importance of our projects." GDS's writing guidance is likewise addressed to everyone publishing on GOV.UK, not to the team that set the standard ([Writing to GOV.UK standards](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/)). **Two independent published standards, so this clears the bar**: the guidelines artifact's characteristic reader is a third party executing on the brand's behalf. That is a different reader from the one `to-brand-identity` has, which is the founder.
- **The verbal execution document is a separate, differently-named genre: the content style guide.** Mailchimp's is the reference implementation and its own source of record ([content-style-guide](https://github.com/mailchimp/content-style-guide/blob/master/02-voice-and-tone.html.md)). Its structure: a voice/tone distinction stated as a rule — "You have the same voice all the time, but your tone changes" — then named voice attributes (plainspoken, genuine, translator, dry humour), then tone rules keyed to the reader's emotional state, then mechanical style rules (active voice, plain English, positive language), then a named exclusion ("Freddie… does not talk. Don't write in his voice"). GDS's equivalent sections are user needs, structure, language, tone, links, titles, summaries and change notes ([writing guidelines](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/)).
- **Tone has a published, checkable structure, and it is a spectrum-set rather than an adjective list.** NN/g's four dimensions, verbatim: "Formal vs. casual", "Serious vs. funny", "Respectful vs. irreverent", "Matter-of-fact vs. enthusiastic" ([The Four Dimensions of Tone of Voice](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/)). NN/g's stated method is to pick a combination, then refine with "more specific tone target words like 'playful,' 'quirky,' or 'sarcastic,'" then — the part that matters most here — select **"anti-tone words"** representing tones to avoid. Mailchimp independently implements the same negative-space move ("We're weird but not inappropriate, smart but not snobbish"), and Wikimedia's guidelines have a literal "Do not…" section. **Clears the bar** across two independent sources plus a published standard, and it is a directly reusable admissibility mechanism: a voice rule that cannot name what it excludes is not a rule.
- **Does not clear the bar: the claim that the execution layer must be its own file rather than a section of a larger one.** No source addresses document granularity. Wheeler's guidelines, Aaker's "communication guide", Mailchimp's style guide and Wikimedia's identity guidelines are four different packagings of overlapping content, and nothing in the literature adjudicates between them. The map should treat file-count as an undecided implementation question, not as something the field settles.
- **Also does not clear the bar: the widely-repeated practitioner critique that guidelines documents fail as rulebooks.** It was searched for deliberately and every retrievable statement of it was agency content. Neumeier comes closest with "A living brand is a pattern of behavior, not a stylistic veneer" ([*The Brand Gap* deck](http://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/images/0321348109/goodies/the_brand_gap.pdf)) — which is suggestive and is *not* a claim about guidelines documents. Recorded as unsourced rather than smoothed over, because it is the argument someone will inevitably make against shipping a `brand-guidelines` artifact and it currently has no citable backing.

## Q5 — The visual brief: separate artifact, or a section?

**Verdict: separate, and Wheeler separates it by hand and by name. This is the clearest single-source statement in the pass and it is corroborated structurally rather than in prose, so it clears the bar weakly. It also settles something the map left open: the brief is project-scoped and disposable, and it is not part of the durable strategy artifact.**

- **Wheeler states the boundary as a negation, which is the useful form.** "The brand brief is not a creative brief" — and the reason is scope: "The creative brief has to do with the project itself, the constraints of that project… We're going to look at three applications" ([Logo Geek interview](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/design-a-brand-identity-with-alina-wheeler/)). Two documents, two lifespans: the brand brief is the compass the designer returns to across every project; the creative brief exists for one engagement and names that engagement's constraints and deliverables. The publisher's contents listing corroborates that both are named chapters ([contents](https://books.google.co.in/books/about/Designing_Brand_Identity.html?hl=en&id=VLg6DwAAQBAJ)).
- **Johnson corroborates structurally.** The half-step "2.5 Bridging the Gap" between "Strategy and Narrative" and "Design" is exactly the translation Wheeler's creative brief performs ([Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/products/branding-in-five-and-a-half-steps)). This is a contents listing, not the author's prose, so it corroborates the *existence* of a translation artifact and not its contents.
- **Clears the bar: weakly.** One practitioner in her own words plus one practitioner's published structure. Enough to say the field distinguishes a project brief from the strategy document; not enough to specify what fields the brief contains. No primary source retrieved enumerates a creative brief's sections, and the professional-body material located ([D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/insights/learning/how-to-break-down-a-brief-new-blood-advice)) is about how a recipient should *read* a brief, not how one is structured.
- **Consequence for the map's "visual reduced to a brief" scope decision:** the scoping is field-correct — Wheeler independently makes the same cut — but the brief is a *different lifecycle* from everything else the skill would produce. It is written for one designer for one engagement. That argues against filing it as a section of a durable artifact and in favour of it being generated on demand, or at minimum dated and scoped to the engagement that occasioned it.

## Q6 — Durable reference or one-off workshop output?

**Verdict: both, and the sources separate them cleanly along a three-way lifecycle. This is the second finding that breaks the two-artifact count.**

- **Durable and rarely changed: the strategy layer.** Collins and Porras's core values are tested against "Would you want your organization to continue to stand for this core value 100 years into the future, no matter what changes occur in the outside world?" and their purpose against "Can you envision this purpose being as valid 100 years from now as it is today?" ([Vision Framework](https://www.jimcollins.com/tools/vision-framework.pdf)). Aaker's brand vision is the aspirational constant against which programs are generated ([The First Step in Building a Brand](https://prophet.com/2014/10/the-first-step-in-building-a-brand/)). Wheeler's brand brief is the document designers return to. Three sources; clears the bar.
- **Revisable: the communicated position.** Aaker's wording is the tell — "the **current** positioning often emphasizes the brand vision elements that are credible and deliverable" ([It Starts With a Brand Vision](https://prophet.com/2014/03/185-it-starts-with-a-brand-vision/)). What is credible today is not what will be credible after the product ships. This is single-sourced as an explicit statement about cadence, so it does **not** clear the bar as a structural claim — but it is the direct answer to the map's open question about whether brand claims freeze or change in place, and it says: *the durable half freezes, the communicated half does not*, which is a different answer from either of the two the map was choosing between.
- **Disposable: the project brief.** Wheeler, per Q5.
- **The workshop/document distinction is a red herring, and Collins and Porras prove it.** Their framework is unambiguously a workshop instrument — "Core Values Breakout Session. Estimated Time: 45 minutes", ending "Write the core values on a flip chart" — and its output is unambiguously durable. A one-off elicitation session producing a permanent record is the normal case, not a contradiction. The instrument itself is durable and reusable; the session is not; the output is.
- **The same source supplies the strongest guard against mush in the entire pass, and it is directly relevant to [#140](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/140).** Collins and Porras print seven yes/no test questions per candidate value, of which the load-bearing one is: "Would you want your organization to hold this core value, **even if at some point in time it became a competitive disadvantage**—even if in some instances the environment penalized the organization for living this core value?" They say so themselves: "the only items that truly qualify as core values as those that meet all of the above test questions with a resounding and unqualified 'Yes!' especially the question 'would you continue to hold it if it were a strategic disadvantage?'" They also print the failure mode the map is worried about, and its cause: "people frequently confuse timeless core values… with aspirations of what you'd like to see the organization become in the future… Do not mix future aspirations into your true and authentic core values, as this will create justifiable cynicism and destroy the power of your core values." And the framing instruction: "this is not a wordsmithing exercise." This is a published, tested-in-print bar and it is the same shape as the map's reversal-test candidate, arrived at independently. **#140 owns the decision; this doc records that the candidate bar has at least one strong published precedent** — and that Neumeier's "onliness", his focus test ("1 Who are you? 2 What do you do? 3 Why does it matter?" — [*The Brand Gap* deck](http://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/images/0321348109/goodies/the_brand_gap.pdf)) and Keller's requirement that a POD be simultaneously desirable, deliverable and differentiating are three further independent instances of the same move.

## Q7 — Which claims clear the two-source bar

Stated flatly, because the map asked for it explicitly.

**Clears:**

1. A short, durable, written strategy-layer artifact is a universal output — Wheeler, Aaker, Neumeier, Keller, Collins/Porras.
2. A separate execution-layer artifact exists downstream of it and is generated from a filtered subset of it — Aaker, Neumeier, Wheeler, Keller, Johnson.
3. "Guidelines" / "standards" is the field's own name for the execution artifact — Wheeler, Aaker, plus four published standards.
4. Seven strategy fields: purpose, values, personality, audience, differentiator, proof, and an optional short essence line.
5. The strategy artifact is short — one page (Wheeler), two to five core elements (Aaker), three to five words for the essence (Keller), three to five values (Collins/Porras).
6. Voice and tone have a checkable published structure, and the mechanism is spectra plus explicit exclusions — NN/g, Mailchimp, Wikimedia.
7. The guidelines artifact's characteristic reader is a third party executing on the brand's behalf — Wikimedia, GDS.
8. A project brief is distinct from the strategy document — Wheeler in her own words, Johnson structurally (weakly).

**Does not clear:**

1. Any particular *name* for the strategy artifact. Six sources, six names, no overlap.
2. "Canon" as a term of art anywhere in the literature — zero occurrences.
3. That the split is two-way. Aaker and Wheeler both describe three layers.
4. Points-of-parity as a field — Keller only.
5. Narrative, value proposition sub-types, and key products as fields — one source each.
6. **Messaging templates as an artifact or a section — no primary source at all.**
7. That the execution layer is its own file rather than a section.
8. The critique that guidelines-as-rulebook fails — retrievable only in inadmissible sources.
9. Kapferer's prism and Olins — named, not scored, primary text not retrievable.

## Bottom line

**The split survives. One of the two names survives. The count does not.**

`brand-guidelines` **survives as a name** — it is the field's own word, used by Wheeler, matched by Aaker's "communication guide", and worn as a title by every published standard consulted. But it survives with a warning attached: the document that the field actually publishes under that name is mostly *visual* rules, addressed to *third parties*, and the map has scoped both of those out. The verbal execution material the skill genuinely owns lives in a differently-named genre — the **content style guide** — with its own well-specified structure (voice attributes, tone spectra, anti-tone words, worked examples, mechanical rules). Adopting `brand-guidelines` for a verbal-only, founder-facing document imports reader expectations the artifact will not meet.

`brand-canon` **does not survive.** It is not a name any source uses, and the six names that are in play do not converge, so there is no incumbent to defeat — the map is free to choose, and should choose on fitness rather than on field precedent. On fitness, "canon" is the same class of error as "Wedge" and "persona": it asserts fixity and completeness over an artifact that Aaker explicitly says has a revisable half, and it asserts authority over an artifact that this repo's own [ADR 0008](https://github.com/98kb/skills/blob/main/docs/adr/0008-stance-record-replaces-persona-as-the-artifact-category.md) standing says must never be an authority. The best-supported alternative is **`brand-brief`** — Wheeler's word, whose field list is corroborated field-by-field by four other practitioners, whose one-page size constraint is independently corroborated three ways, and whose connotations are exactly right: a brief is short, it is a compass rather than a rulebook, and it is unembarrassed about being incomplete.

**The count is the real finding, and it is the thing this pass overturns.** The literature draws two lines, not one. Aaker's is the cleanest: **brand vision** (durable, internal, six to twelve elements, two to five of them core) → **brand position** (the credible-and-deliverable subset, revisable, external-facing) → **communication guide** (execution). Wheeler's is the same shape with a different third term: **brand brief** (durable, one page) → **guidelines** (published at launch) → **creative brief** (project-scoped, disposable). A two-artifact design has to put the revisable, external-facing positioning layer somewhere, and both available homes are wrong — in the strategy artifact it contaminates a frozen document with something that changes, and in the guidelines artifact it buries a strategy decision inside a style manual.

**Where the sources come up empty, recorded rather than smoothed.** There is no published method behind "messaging templates" — the search returned nothing admissible, and the map should treat any messaging section as an invention of this repo requiring its own justification rather than as a borrowing. There is no retrievable statement of the standard practitioner critique of guidelines documents, which is awkward, because it is the most likely objection to the artifact and it cannot currently be answered from sources. Kapferer's prism and Olins's work were both named and both unreadable in the original, so a seventh and eighth field vocabulary exist that this pass did not score. And the strongest evidence in the pass is not about artifact structure at all: it is Collins and Porras's seven printed test questions, which are a working, published, thirty-year-old solution to precisely the mush problem the map identifies as load-bearing — and they belong to [#140](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/140), not here.
