# What kind of document is a recorded stance — how does it stay descriptive rather than prescriptive, what would make it checkably wrong, and is it frozen or living?

Research for [#83](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/83), under [Map: to-persona Phase 1 (#81)](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/81); covers [#80](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/80)'s Q4, Q5 and Q3 in that order. The artifact under design is a durable record of a founder's stance, loaded as **context enrichment** for whoever works downstream. It is reference material and never an authority: every conflict is resolved by a human, and an agent must never derive a decision from it. The hard case throughout is a reader — increasingly an agent, holding the document silently on every question — consulting it for a question it never contemplated. Sourcing bar is the same as `vision-artifact-shape.md` and `riskiest-assumption-falsifiability.md`: professional and statutory standards, published method texts, and first-party practitioner writing only. Secondary write-ups were used to locate primary URLs and are cited nowhere.

## Q1 — How do these disciplines mark the boundary of what a record covers, for a reader holding it for a question it never contemplated?

**Verdict: four independent disciplines converge on the same mechanism, and it is not a disclaimer. The boundary is marked by indexing each entry to the question it actually answered, so "this record is silent here" becomes a structurally detectable condition rather than a judgement call — reinforced by a per-entry cap on how far a claim may reach past its own stated basis. Design personas, the discipline #80 expected the most from, have no such mechanism at all.**

- **Judicial — the boundary is enforced by the reader, keyed to the question presented.** *Webster v. Fall*: "The most that can be said is that the point was in the cases if any one had seen fit to raise it. Questions which merely lurk in the record, neither brought to the attention of the court nor ruled upon, are not to be considered as having been so decided as to constitute precedents." Note what carries the weight: nothing in the earlier opinion disclaims anything. The rule works only because a case record shows the question that was presented, which makes silence on an unpresented question visible rather than inferable.
- **Evidentiary — purpose-scoping is real, and its own authors concede the disclaimer form fails.** Fed. R. Evid. 105 requires a court that admits evidence for one purpose but not another to "restrict the evidence to its proper scope and instruct the jury accordingly" — but the Advisory Committee Note attached to that same rule records that limiting instructions are not always effective, citing *Bruton v. United States*. This is primary-source evidence, from inside the discipline, that telling a reader to limit their use of a document in front of them is the weak form of the mechanism. It bears directly on the agent case.
- **Evidentiary — separation is done by field, not by label.** Fed. R. Evid. 701 confines a non-expert's opinion to one "rationally based on the witness's perception" and "not based on scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge within the scope of Rule 702"; the 2000 Advisory Committee Note distinguishes reasoning "familiar in everyday life" from reasoning "which can be mastered only by specialists." Report and inference are kept apart by which slot a statement is allowed to occupy.
- **Clinical — every entry is bound to an enumerated question.** POMR is explicit: "For each problem defined, a SOAP note must be recorded." Nothing is charted at large; each note attaches to a numbered problem on the Problem List. The reach of each entry is capped by rule — "A problem should be defined at its highest level of defensibility" — with a worked example in which listing six abnormalities and calling them "uremia" are *both* acceptable Problem Lists, differing only in the level of inference the author is willing to defend (POMR, §2.A). Within the note, "The Subjective and the Objective are each a brief review of the abnormalities identified in the history, physical, and initial lab data," while "The Assessment is a brief but pertinent paragraph describing what the physician thinks about that particular problem" — data and clinician inference occupy different fields.
- **Clinical — the hardest case has a direct answer: absence is a first-class dated entry.** POMR §2.D: "If the initial data base is incomplete, the Problem List must state so," with the worked example entering "Incomplete Data Base / Pelvic/Paps Not Done" as its own numbered, dated problem, later closed with its own date. This is the strongest single finding in the pass. Nobody can enumerate every question a founder never addressed — but the questions a session *opened and failed to close* are enumerable, and this discipline makes recording them mandatory rather than optional prose.
- **Professional standards — per-claim provenance, and a duty to limit conclusions.** APA Ethics 9.01(b) requires that where an examination is impractical, psychologists "document the efforts they made and the result of those efforts, clarify the probable impact of their limited information on the reliability and validity of their opinions, and appropriately limit the nature and extent of their conclusions or recommendations"; 9.06 requires them to "indicate any significant limitations of their interpretations." The Forensic Guidelines push this to the item level: practitioners are to "identify the source of each piece of information that was considered and relied upon in formulating a particular conclusion" (11.03), and reports should give "the basis and reasoning underlying the opinions, the salient data or other information that was considered" (11.04). A conclusion whose basis is not recorded is a standards violation, not a stylistic lapse.
- **Professional standards — context is a mandatory field precisely because meaning drifts.** APA Record Keeping Guideline 8: "information in a client's record is specific to a given temporal or situational context (e.g., the time frame and situation in which the services were delivered and the records were created). When that context changes over time, the relevance and meaning of the information may also change. Preserving the context of the record protects the client from the misuse or misinterpretation of those data in a way that could prejudice or harm the client." Its application warns that behaviour recorded under stress "may not represent the client's enduring psychological characteristics." That is the founder case exactly: a position taken under one set of constraints is not a general disposition, and the countermeasure is a per-entry context field, not a preamble.
- **Archival — a standards body writing an anti-extrapolation rule about its own document.** ISAD(G) I.12: "Examples throughout the text of ISAD(G) are illustrative and not prescriptive. They illuminate the provisions of the rules to which they are attached, rather than extend those provisions. Do not take the examples, or the form in which they are presented as instructions." The mechanism is *attachment* — each example is bound to one rule and reaches no further. ISAD(G) 2.2 states the same discipline for description generally: "Provide only such information as is appropriate to the level being described." Every description must also declare its own level of description (I.12's essential elements), and how the description itself was made is quarantined in a separate Description Control Area, "where information is conveyed on how, when and by whom the archival description was prepared" (I.11).
- **Archival — disclosure of the describer, not a pose of neutrality.** DACS Principle 5: "Archival description must be clear about what archivists know, what they don't know, and how they know it." Principle 6: "Archivists must document and make discoverable the actions they take on records." The standard explicitly rejects the idea that archivists are impartial stenographers and answers it with disclosure.
- **Provenance modelling — the founder-said/reader-inferred split has a carryable form.** PROV-O defines derivation as a first-class relation: `wasDerivedFrom` is "a transformation of an entity into another, an update of an entity resulting in a new one, or the construction of a new entity based on a pre-existing entity," and `wasAttributedTo` separately ascribes an entity to an agent. An extrapolation is therefore representable as a *new* entity derived from the record and attributed to the extrapolator — never merged into, and never attributed to, the founder. This is the only source found that gives the distinction a shape an agent can carry rather than merely respect.
- **Personas — a genuine empty result, and it should be reported as one.** NN/g's own definition is "a fictional, yet realistic, description of a typical or target user of the product," and their failure-mode article names adoption problems — personas "created, but not used," no leadership buy-in, "created in isolation," communication failure — and does not name over-extension beyond the evidence at all. Indi Young's first-party critique lands closer, warning that invented detail lets "cognitive bias creep in" and that solution-anchored personas produce backward reasoning, but her remedy changes the *content* (thinking styles built from listening sessions: "people's inner voice," reactions, guiding principles) rather than bounding the description's reach. Persona practice has no boundary mechanism to borrow.

## Q2 — What is the falsifiability analogue? What would make a recorded stance checkably wrong?

**Verdict: no third-party-applicable truth test exists, and the sources agree on why — a record of a person is answerable to its stated basis and its subject, not to the world. This is a real finding, not a gap: it relocates the eval seam exactly as #81 anticipated. Two checks survive: a provenance-and-scope audit anyone can run, and a subject-resolved prediction test only the founder can resolve.**

- **Oral history explicitly declines the correspondence test.** OHA's guidance for users asks them to recognise "the subjectivity of the interview, including, when possible, verification of information presented as factual" — verification is an external activity performed on the account, not a property the record carries. The record's own integrity standard is fidelity instead: the field "prioritizes preserving the original recording of the interview" (Archiving Oral History), and the ethics statement requires that "the narrator be given the opportunity to review the interview (recording and/or transcript) and approve what was said for public release." Accuracy-to-the-subject replaces truth-about-the-world.
- **HIPAA gives subject attestation a legal form — and notably it is a dispute channel, not a truth channel.** Where an amendment is denied, the individual may "submit to the covered entity a written statement disagreeing with the denial of all or part of a requested amendment and the basis of such disagreement"; the entity "may prepare a written rebuttal"; both are appended to the record and travel with any subsequent disclosure (45 CFR 164.526(d)). The record is never required to be right. It is required to carry its own contest. That is the closest statutory analogue to falsifiability for a record about a person, and it deliberately settles nothing.
- **The third-party-applicable check is a provenance-and-scope audit, not an accuracy test.** Forensic Guideline 11.03's per-conclusion sourcing, APA 9.01(b)'s duty to "appropriately limit the nature and extent of their conclusions," and POMR's "highest level of defensibility" are all runnable by a reader with no access to the founder: does each recorded position name the concrete decision it was drawn from, and does the claim it makes stay at that level? A position that outruns its own recorded basis is wrong in the only sense checkable without the subject. This is what a deterministic grader can grade.
- **The subject-resolved test has a working analogue in forecasting.** GJ Open's discipline is that the resolution conditions and the resolution authority are fixed *before* the outcome is known — outcomes determined "using credible, open-source evidence and media reporting," questions resolved on data as initially released with "subsequent revisions to the data" excluded, and ambiguous cases voided rather than argued. Transposed: a recorded position is falsifiable iff a third party can derive from it, before the founder answers, a prediction of how the founder will decide a live question inside that position's stated scope — with the founder named as the resolution authority. Cheap, genuinely disconfirmable, and not runnable by the third party alone.
- **The negative form is the only Popperian shape available, and #80 already found it.** "Positions this person would never take" is a universal negative: one counter-instance refutes it, with no threshold, no rubric, and no judge. Every other component of a stance is a description that can only be more or less apt; a never-list is the one component that can be flatly wrong.
- **Decision journals supply the missing half — the check is a scheduled comparison, not a property of the document.** The method records "what you expect to happen and the reasoning" at the time, and its value is that it "helps combat [hindsight bias] by recording what you knew and what you thought at the time," with the learning found in the gap on later review. Nothing about the entry makes it checkable; the *review ritual* does.

## Q3 — Is such a record kept true over time, or deliberately point-in-time?

**Verdict: unanimous among the record-keeping disciplines, and against in-place revision. Every one of them fixes the entry at creation and handles change by dated addition. The single dissenting practice is design personas, which revise and retire freely — and which are not records of a person. Recommendation: freeze and append; do not port `to-vision`'s in-place-revision-clears-approval machinery.**

- **The profession states the prohibition directly.** APA Record Keeping Guidelines, under *Alteration or destruction of records*: "Many statutes, regulations, and rules of evidence prohibit the alteration or removal of information once a record has been made... It is recommended that later additions made to a record be documented as such."
- **HIPAA operationalises it in the hardest case — the one where everyone agrees the record was wrong.** An *accepted* amendment is executed by "identifying the records in the designated record set that are affected by the amendment and appending or otherwise providing a link to the location of the amendment" (§164.526(c)). Even an agreed correction does not overwrite. This is the sharpest available refutation of an in-place-revision mechanism for a record about a person.
- **POMR shows append-only staying legible, not just compliant.** Problems carry an entry date and a resolution date, and the resolution date "refers an observer to that date's progress note which will explain why the problem is considered resolved"; a refined problem is shown as `BUN → Uremia → Secondary to membranous glomerulonephropathy` with a date on each arrow. Supersession is a dated cross-reference and the superseded entry stays readable. The record grows without any entry being edited.
- **Oral history is the strongest point-in-time case.** The field "prioritizes preserving the original recording of the interview," and archiving practice preserves the surrounding provenance — "oral history project proposals, correspondence between interviewers and narrators, interviewer notes" — as part of the accession. A narrator's later change of mind is a new interview, not an edit; the whole value of the artifact is that it is dated.
- **Archival practice splits frozen from living along a line worth copying exactly.** ISAD(G) I.3 says the information about material "remains dynamic and may be subject to amendment in the light of further knowledge of its content or the context of its creation," and DACS Principle 11 says "Archival description is a continuous intellectual endeavor" — but both apply that to the *description*, never to the record described, and ISAD(G) quarantines the description's own authorship in the Description Control Area. Transposed: stance entries are the record and are frozen; any index, summary or navigational layer over them is description and may be revised, with its own authorship recorded separately.
- **The dissent, and why it loses.** Personas are revised and retired without ceremony, but NN/g's own definition is of "a fictional, yet realistic, description of a typical or target user" — a design target, not a record of an identified person's stated position — and their failure-mode analysis concerns adoption, not currency. The sources genuinely disagree; the disagreement resolves on what the artifact is, and a founder stance is a record of a person at a time.
- **A cheap complementary device, from a source that applies it to itself.** The APA Record Keeping Guidelines carry an explicit sunset: "These guidelines are scheduled to expire 10 years from February 16, 2007... After this date, users are encouraged to contact the APA Practice Directorate to determine whether this document remains in effect." An expiry forces a reader to re-check; a silent revision hides the change from them.

## Bottom line

1. **Adopt the boundary mechanism: entries indexed by the decision they were elicited about.** Each recorded position is one entry, and each entry names the concrete decision or case it came from — POMR's "for each problem defined, a SOAP note must be recorded," and *Webster v. Fall*'s rule that questions which "merely lurk in the record" were never decided. This is the answer to the agent case specifically: "no entry matches this question" is a computable condition an agent can check, whereas "does this stance extend to X" is a judgement it will silently make wrong. A topic-indexed prose document cannot support this check; a question-indexed one can.
2. **Adopt a dated open-questions register as a first-class section.** POMR §2.D — "If the initial data base is incomplete, the Problem List must state so" — entered as a numbered, dated problem and closed with a date. The unaddressed-question set is infinite, but the set the session raised and did not close is finite and enumerable, and recording it is the only found mechanism that makes silence visible in advance rather than discoverable by a reader who already went wrong.
3. **Adopt a per-entry basis, context, and defensibility cap.** Every position carries the situation it was formed in (APA Record Keeping Guideline 8), the source of the claim (Forensic 11.03), and a claim pitched no higher than the evidence supports (POMR "highest level of defensibility"; APA 9.01(b)'s duty to "appropriately limit the nature and extent of their conclusions"). Keep what the founder said and what the recorder concluded in different fields, per FRE 701 and POMR's S/O-versus-A split.
4. **Adopt derivation as a modelled relation, not a warning.** An extrapolation is a new entry, `wasDerivedFrom` the founder's entry and `wasAttributedTo` whoever made it (PROV-O). This is what #80 asked for when it demanded "a mechanism that makes the second one visibly not the first."
5. **Reject the disclaimer, on primary-source grounds.** FRE 105's own Advisory Committee Note concedes that instructing a reader to restrict a document's use sometimes fails outright. And reject design personas as a source of boundary machinery: NN/g and Indi Young between them name no mechanism for bounding a persona's reach. That is an empty result worth recording, since #80 expected this to be the richest territory.
6. **Falsifiability verdict: there is no third-party-applicable test, and the eval seam must move.** What replaces it is two things. (a) A **provenance-and-scope audit**, runnable by anyone and deterministically gradeable: does every entry name its originating decision, state its scope, and stay at its stated level of inference? (b) A **founder-resolved prediction test**, borrowed from forecasting practice: a position is checkably wrong if a third party can predict from it, in advance, how the founder will decide a live question inside its scope, and the founder — the named resolution authority — decides the other way. Only the never-list is Popperian outright: one counter-instance kills it. Consequence for [ADR 0003](https://github.com/98kb/skills/blob/main/docs/adr/0003-eval-grading-is-deterministic-plus-llm-judge-split.md): the deterministic half survives and grows (structure, provenance, scope, register completeness); the judge half cannot grade accuracy at all and should not be asked to. Accuracy belongs to a founder-run review ritual, not to an eval suite.
7. **Frozen, with append-only revision — do not copy `to-vision`.** `to-vision` and `to-pitch` update the artifact "in place with the change — same mechanism, no parallel version history" and clear `approved_by`/`approved_at`. Every record-keeping source consulted forbids exactly that for a record of a person, including HIPAA in the case where the record is agreed to be wrong. Concretely: a later sitting appends new dated entries; a superseded position gets a dated cross-reference to the entry that supersedes it and stays legible; approval attaches per entry and is permanent, because an approved entry is never edited, which removes the reason `to-vision` had to clear approval at all. Optionally give the document a stated re-check date, as the APA Record Keeping Guidelines do for themselves.
8. **Where the sources are thin.** Nothing in the corpus addresses a record read by a non-human reader, so every mechanism above is transposed rather than tested for that case; FRE 105's admission that limiting instructions fail on *human* juries is the best available warning that the transposition is if anything too optimistic. And the frozen-versus-living finding rests on records of people; the one discipline that revises freely (personas) is excluded on the grounds that its object is a design target, which is a judgement about what `to-persona` is, and is the point where this recommendation is most contestable.
