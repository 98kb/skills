# Is "persona" the right artifact category for a recorded founder stance, or does another established category fit its constraints better?

Research for [#87](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/87), under [Map: to-persona Phase 1 (#81)](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/81). The name `persona` was inherited from [#80](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/80)'s working title and has never been tested. A category is not a label: it drags in standard sections, reader expectations, and a set of failure modes the reader will assume apply. Three completed passes supply the constraints scored below — [#82](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/82) (decision-informing components; impersonation excluded), [#83](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/83) (question-indexed entries, frozen/append-only, never an authority), [#84](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/84) (a ~200-line always-loaded index over per-question retrievable positions). The hypothesis under test is that a persona describes a *fictional composite of users* as a *design target* while this artifact records *one real named decision-maker* as a *historical record* — and that if so, persona machinery is the wrong genre rather than surplus. Sourcing bar is the same as the sibling docs: originating papers, statutory and model-act text with official comments, published method texts, and first-party practitioner writing. Where a category's primary text was not retrievable it is named and not scored, rather than scored off a secondary summary.

## Q1 — What are the candidate categories, and which are serious enough to score?

**Verdict: sixteen were examined; nine are serious. The eliminations are decided by each category's own statement of what its object is, and the single most common disqualifier is that the object is not a person at all.**

- **Eliminated on their own definition of the object.** *Archetype*: NN/g say personas and archetypes "represent the same data and insights about our users' behaviors, attitudes, goals, and pain points," differing only in whether "we invent a (plausible) name, bio, photo" — so an archetype is a persona with the invented layer deleted, and inherits every other persona property. The ticket's suspicion is confirmed by NN/g themselves. *Thinking styles*: Indi Young's own framing is "grouping people by *how they think*" — a demographics-free grouping across many people, still an aggregate and still a design target. *Jobs-to-be-done*: Ulwick's unit is "a group of people plus the core functional job," and "jobs are stable; products are not" — the job is deliberately independent of any individual. *Competency model*: the object is a role. McClelland's BEI survives as [#82](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/82)'s elicitation instrument; the competency dictionary it feeds is not this artifact.
- **Named and not scored, for lack of retrievable primary text.** MacLean, Young, Bellotti & Moran's **QOC** and its Design Space Analysis are behind Taylor & Francis and ACM paywalls; every open copy found was a secondary summary. QOC is a sibling of IBIS and DRL and would not change the ranking, but it is recorded here as unscored rather than scored on hearsay.
- **The nine scored:** design persona (the incumbent); IBIS in Kunz & Rittel's original form; DRL/SIBYL; the architecture decision record; the problem-oriented medical record; oral history / testimony; the deposition; the published principles or credo register; the advance health-care directive. Two supporting categories are scored as *mechanism donors* rather than as genres: the commonplace book and the ALI Restatement.
- **Added by the sources themselves, and the most consequential addition in this pass.** The **advance health-care instruction** was not on the ticket's list and is the closest match found on purpose. The Uniform Law Commission defines it as "a direction, whether or not in a record, made by an individual that indicates the individual's goals, preferences, or wishes concerning the individual's health care" (UHCDA 2023 §2(13)) — one real named subject, recording positions in advance, read later by a surrogate facing a decision the subject is no longer able to make.

## Q2 — Scored against the six constraints

Constraints, from the prior passes: **C1** one real named subject; **C2** decision-informing, not impersonating; **C3** question-indexed, so "no entry covers this" is computable; **C4** frozen / append-only; **C5** reference material, never an authority; **C6** small always-loaded index over retrievable entries.

**Verdict: no category clears all six, and the two that come closest fail on opposite halves. The incumbent scores worst of the nine.**

- **Design persona — 1 of 6.** C1 fails by definition (NN/g: "a fictional, yet realistic, description of a typical or target user"). C2 fails: [#82](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/82) established the invented layer is losslessly deletable yet colonises the artifact because it is trivially elicitable. C3 fails: [#83](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/83) found personas have *no* boundary mechanism at all. C4 fails: personas are revised and retired freely. C6 fails: a persona is one narrative document. Only C5 survives, and only because nobody claims a persona binds anyone.
- **IBIS (Kunz & Rittel) — 4 of 6, and it owns the hardest constraint.** C3 is satisfied by construction: "Issues have the form of questions," "Issues are specific to particular situations," and the elements are "topics, issues, questions of fact, positions, arguments, and model problems." C5 is enforced structurally rather than by warning — Conklin & Begeman: "There is no stopping rule, nor is there in the IBIS method a particular way of registering that an Issue has been resolved by agreement upon some Position." C4 holds (the issue bank grows; issues are "raised, argued, settled, 'dodged,' or substituted"). C6 holds in spirit — Kunz & Rittel's stated retrieval problem is [#84](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/84)'s lexical-findability result arrived at fifty-four years earlier: the discourse "is notoriously centering around concepts expressed by ad hoc vocabulary which is rapidly changing, defying thesaurization," so what matters is "the similarity of an issue with issues dealt with previously," not a librarian's subject terms. **C1 fails outright**: IBIS's subject is a *cooperative* — "governmental or administrative agencies or committees, planning groups" — never a named individual.
- **Advance health-care instruction — 4 of 6, on the opposite four.** C1, C2 and C4 are clean, and the purpose match is exact. The drafting committee's own Comment states this artifact's central design risk verbatim: "instructions included in advance directives focus unduly on preferences for specific treatments, and do not provide health-care professionals or surrogates with the type of information about patients' goals and values that could be used to make value-congruent decisions when **novel or unexpected situations arise**" — and the 2023 fix is to add goals and values alongside specific preferences, which independently vindicates [#82](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/82)'s component set. **C5 fails head-on**: an instruction *directs*. C3 fails: it is indexed by treatment, not by question. C6 was never a design consideration.
- **Architecture decision record — 3 of 6, and it owns C4.** Nygard's supersession rule is exactly what [#83](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/83) derived from HIPAA and POMR: "If a decision is reversed, we will keep the old one around, but mark it as superseded." His audience framing is also right: "a conversation with a future developer." But C1 fails (the subject is a system, and the voice is deliberately collective — "stated in full sentences, with active voice. 'We will …'"), and **C5 fails hardest of any category scored**: an ADR records a decision that binds. Naming a stance an ADR converts a preference into a commitment by genre.
- **POMR — 4 of 6, and it is already the adopted mechanism donor.** One named subject, problem-indexed entries, append-only, clinician-decides. It fails as a *genre* because its subject is the object of care rather than the author of a position, and because C6 was never contemplated. [#83](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/83) already took its mechanisms; nothing here changes that.
- **Oral history / testimony — 3 of 6.** C1, C4 and C5 are clean and it is the strongest point-in-time case in the corpus. C3 and C6 fail hard: a transcript is narrative, unindexed by question, and far past any context budget.
- **Deposition — 3 of 6, with one mechanism worth stealing.** FRCP 30(e) lets the deponent "sign a statement listing the changes and the reasons for making them," with the changes *attached* rather than merged — an independent second discipline landing on HIPAA §164.526(c)'s append-only correction. Question-and-answer form satisfies C3 mechanically. But it is adversarial, sworn, produced for impeachment, and C5 fails: a deposition is evidence.
- **Principles / credo register — 2 of 6, and it is the most dangerous mislabel available.** Amazon's own framing is decision-authority: "We use our Leadership Principles every day, whether we're discussing ideas for new projects or deciding on the best way to solve a problem." Dalio universalises: "Principles are ways of successfully dealing with reality to get what you want out of life." C5 fails by genre, C4 fails (these are living documents, revised in place), C3 fails (numbered imperatives, not questions). C6 passes, which is precisely the trap — the shape is convenient and the standing is wrong.
- **DRL/SIBYL — 2 of 6.** Lee's vocabulary is Alternatives, Goals, a Decision Problem, and Claims whose plausibility is argued; the object is a decision problem, not a person. Its one transferable idea is that SIBYL manages "viewpoints" and "precedents" as first-class services — per-holder attribution inside a rationale record, which is where a *named* subject could attach.
- **Mechanism donors.** Locke's commonplace book is [#84](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/84)'s two-tier shape in 1706: an index in which "upon these Two Letters depends the whole Use of our Index," over pages appended in order of occurrence and never reorganised. It fails on substance twice — the entries are other people's words, and the heads are *topics*, which is the exact indexing [#83](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/83) showed cannot make silence computable. The ALI Restatement donates the standing this artifact needs and has no name for: black letter and Comments are approved, while "the Reporter's Notes are regarded as the work of the Reporter and are not approved by ALI members," and courts adopt Restatement sections not because they must but because they are persuasive. That is "reference material, never an authority" as an established legal category — **persuasive authority** — with a matching structural split between the approved record and the recorder's attributed layer, which is PROV-O's derivation rule in a second form.

## Q3 — What each name drags in: standard sections, and the failure modes the reader will assume

**Verdict: this is where "persona" is worst, and the mechanism is the one [#82](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/82) named — the sections a category implies get filled because they are trivially fillable, not because anyone decided to fill them.**

- **Persona** implies name, photo, biography, demographics, a pull-quote, goals, frustrations, a day-in-the-life scenario. Known failure modes: the fictional layer that makes the artifact unfalsifiable (Chapman & Milham), degradation to stereotype (Cooper), and NN/g's created-but-not-used adoption failure. Every one of these is an *impersonation* failure, and [#80](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/80) already excluded impersonation by construction — which means the name is importing the exact machinery the map excluded.
- **Archetype** implies the same sections minus the face, and NN/g's own warning is that removing it "doesn't fix the problem; instead, they simply reinforce the team's implicit biases."
- **IBIS / design rationale** implies issues, positions, arguments, and supports/objects-to links. Its failure modes are documented by its own inventors, and they are *process* costs rather than artifact defects: "cognitive overhead," "premature structuring," and above all the literacy problem — "the effort required to think and represent hypertextually is comparable to the development of fluency in a new language." Their resolution matters here: what made capture work was not a better notation but "a skilled person … fluent with the formalisms," a facilitator who does the structuring so the participants do not have to.
- **ADR** implies Title, Status, Context, Decision, Consequences — and implies bindingness. In this repo it also collides with an existing artifact class under `docs/adr/`.
- **Principles / credo** implies a numbered imperative list, implies prescription, and implies revision in place.
- **Advance directive** implies a treatment-preference checklist, a named surrogate, and a signature. Its failure modes are enumerated and measured, and they are the sharpest warning in this pass — see Q4.
- **Deposition** implies caption, oath, verbatim Q&A, objections, errata sheet — and implies an adversary.
- **Oral history** implies a narrative transcript, a deed of gift, and a length nobody will load.

## Q4 — Does a category fit, or is this a genuine gap?

**Verdict: a genuine gap, and a narrow, describable one. The five properties this artifact needs each exist in an established category; no established category has more than four of them, and the two four-property matches fail on disjoint halves. Say "gap" plainly rather than forcing a fit — but note that the nearest neighbour on *purpose* is the one whose failure modes have been measured and are bad.**

- **The gap, stated precisely.** Object = one real named person's positions (advance directive, oral history, decision journal). Unit and index = a question with positions attached (IBIS). Lifecycle = frozen, superseded by dated append (ADR, POMR, HIPAA, FRCP 30(e)). Standing = persuasive, never binding, with the recorder's inferences attributed separately (Restatement). Access = a small index over retrievable entries (Locke; [#84](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/84)). No category in the corpus combines the first two.
- **The nearest neighbour on purpose is the advance health-care instruction — and it should be studied, not adopted.** Fagerlin & Schneider ask "what would it take for a regime of living wills to function as their advocates hope?" and answer with five conditions: "First, people must have living wills. Second, they must decide what treatment they would want if incompetent. Third, they must accurately and lucidly state that preference. Fourth, their living wills must be available to people making decisions for a patient. Fifth, those people must grasp and heed the living will's instructions. These conditions are unmet and largely unmeetable." Their review found "not one condition has been achieved or, we think, can be." Three of their mechanisms transfer directly and are unaffected by the never-an-authority constraint: preferences are unstable ("over periods as short as two years, almost one-third of preferences for life-sustaining medical treatment changed") — the same expressed-versus-revealed gap [#82](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/82) flagged on protected values, now with an effect size; recorded instructions are vague ("the contents of ADs were vague and difficult to apply to current clinical situations"); and vagueness is not neutral, because "the imprecision of living wills not only stymies interpreters, it exacerbates their natural tendency to read documents in light of their own preferences." Their diagnosis of *why* is the general case, and it is the strongest single argument against this artifact anywhere in Phase 1's evidence: writing instructions for the future requires you to "(1) work out exactly what rule they want, (2) imagine all the circumstances in which it might apply, and (3) find language to specify all those but only those circumstances. Each task is ultimately impossible." **[#86](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/86) should treat this paragraph as go/no-go evidence, not as a design note.**
- **The nearest neighbour to *build on* is the IBIS issue record, and the reason is which failure modes you can pay for.** Adopting the advance-directive genre imports pathologies that are intrinsic to it and that this artifact would have to solve from scratch. Adopting the IBIS genre imports capture overhead, premature structuring, and a literacy requirement — costs that Conklin's own team resolved with a facilitator, and an interview-shaped skill *is* the facilitator. IBIS also supplies the one constraint no other category enforces structurally: with no stopping rule and no way to mark an issue resolved, the record cannot become an authority even if a reader wants it to.
- **What it would have to borrow, and from where.** A single named subject with per-entry attribution — oral history and PROV-O ([#83](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/83)). Frozen-plus-supersede-by-dated-append — Nygard's ADR rule, corroborated by POMR, HIPAA §164.526(c) and FRCP 30(e). Persuasive-not-binding standing, with the recorder's inference in a separately attributed layer — the Restatement's black-letter/Reporter's-Notes split. A dated open-questions register — POMR §2.D. A small routing index over retrievable entries — Locke, and [#84](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/84)'s 200-line budget. Nothing needs to be borrowed from persona practice, which is consistent with [#83](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/83)'s empty result there.
- **The hypothesis under test survives, with one correction.** The category difference is real: the incumbent scores 1 of 6 and fails on the object, the boundary, the lifecycle *and* the loading shape. But the failure modes [#82](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/82) and [#83](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/83) found do not *all* follow from the mismatch. The impersonation gravity and the missing boundary mechanism do. The expressed-versus-revealed problem does not — Fagerlin & Schneider show it recurring in a category with a real named subject, no fiction, and legal force, which means it is a property of self-recorded future-directed preference and would survive any renaming.

## Q5 — What follows for the name

**Verdict: drop "persona". The replacement the evidence supports is already in the map's own vocabulary, arrived at independently — `stance`, whose retrievable unit is a `position` keyed to a `question`.**

- **The convergence is worth stating on its own.** [#84](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/84) named the retrievable unit a **position file** and the always-loaded tier a **stance index**, from a source base of agent-memory and retrieval papers with no exposure to design rationale. Kunz & Rittel's element list is "topics, issues, questions of fact, **positions**, arguments," and gIBIS defines it precisely: "A Position is a statement or assertion which resolves the Issue." [#80](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/80)'s prior art used the same noun twice unprompted ("the positions themselves," "positions this person would never take"). Three disjoint literatures and the map's own drafts already agree on the unit; only the container was misnamed.
- **Concretely:** `to-persona` → `to-stance`; the artifact is a **stance record**; its entries are **positions**, each keyed to the **question** it was elicited about; the always-loaded tier is the **stance index**. [#81](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/81)'s title and [#80](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/80)'s vocabulary go with it. Renaming now costs an issue edit; renaming after Phase 2 costs a skill directory, an eval suite and a composition rule.
- **Names to refuse explicitly, and why**: *persona* and *archetype* (impersonation sections arrive by gravity); *profile* (implies psychometrics and a completeness claim nothing supports); *principles* or *credo* (prescriptive and authoritative by genre — the C5 collision); *founder decision record* or any ADR echo (implies bindingness, and collides with `docs/adr/`); *charter*, *doctrine*, *playbook* (all imperative).
- **The honest cost of naming a gap.** "Stance record" is not an established category, so it inherits no machinery *and* no reader expectations. Every convention — the question key, the basis field, the open-questions register, the supersession marker — has to be stated by the skill and checked by the eval, because no reader will supply them by habit. That is the price of the finding, and it should be paid deliberately rather than discovered in Phase 2.

## Bottom line

**Keep-or-drop: drop "persona."** It scores 1 of 6 against the constraints the prior passes established, and it fails on the object, the boundary mechanism, the lifecycle and the loading shape — not on execution. The hypothesis [#87](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/87) asked to test is confirmed on the sources' own definitions: NN/g's persona is "a fictional, yet realistic, description of a typical or target user," which is a design target composed from many people, while this artifact records one real named person as a historical record.

Ranked verdict on the serious candidates:

1. **IBIS issue record** (Kunz & Rittel 1970; Conklin & Begeman 1988) — 4/6, and it owns the two hardest constraints: question-indexed entries with positions attached, and never-an-authority enforced structurally by having no stopping rule. Fails only on subject: IBIS's subject is a committee. **Nearest neighbour to build on.**
2. **Advance health-care instruction** (UHCDA 2023 §2(13)) — 4/6, and the closest match on *purpose* found anywhere. Fails on standing (it directs) and on indexing. Its drafting committee names this artifact's central risk in its own words, and Fagerlin & Schneider have measured what happens when the genre is deployed at scale. **Study it; do not adopt it.**
3. **POMR** — 4/6, already mined by [#83](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/83); a mechanism donor, not a genre.
4. **ADR** (Nygard) — 3/6; donates the supersession rule and the future-reader framing; disqualified by bindingness and by an existing collision in this repo.
5. **Oral history** — 3/6; donates freezing and subject attestation; unusable shape.
6. **Deposition** (FRCP 30(e)) — 3/6; donates an append-only correction mechanism that independently corroborates HIPAA's.
7. **DRL/SIBYL** — 2/6; donates viewpoints and precedents as first-class relations.
8. **Principles / credo register** — 2/6; the most dangerous near-miss, because the shape fits and the standing is exactly wrong.
9. **Design persona** — 1/6. Drop.

Not scored: QOC (primary text paywalled; named rather than guessed at). Eliminated on their own definitions: archetype, thinking styles, jobs-to-be-done, competency model — all four have a non-individual object.

**This artifact sits in a real gap.** Five properties, five different categories, no category holding more than four. The replacement name the evidence supports is **`to-stance`**, producing a **stance record** of **positions** keyed to **questions** — chosen because three disjoint literatures and the map's own drafts already converged on "position" as the unit while only the container was misnamed, not because "stance" is an established genre. It is not one, and that has a cost: the skill must supply every convention itself and the eval must check them.

**Where the sources disagree or come up empty, recorded rather than smoothed.** QOC could not be read in the original. The design-rationale tradition and the advance-directive tradition disagree about whether such a record should ever settle anything, and the disagreement is not resolvable from outside — it turns on whether the reader has authority to act, which for `to-persona` is settled by [#80](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/80)'s hard constraint rather than by any source. And the strongest evidence in this pass cuts against the artifact rather than for it: Fagerlin & Schneider's finding that not one of the five conditions for a working future-directed personal record "has been achieved or, we think, can be" is the best-measured evidence Phase 1 has produced about anything, and it is about the category this artifact most resembles. It survives renaming. [#86](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/86) should weigh it against the go/no-go, not against the name.
