## 1. The seed cut yields **zero** admissible positions. The whole record is register.

Five candidates cleared ADR 0009's altitude test — each names a trade-off and
each survives having the product artifacts stripped out. **Every one failed the
episode bar.** The seed stance index has **no routing lines** and the register has
**five open entries**.

#85 predicted the register would be "the largest output of this cut." It is the
*only* output. The gap between "mostly" and "all" is not rhetorical, and §2
explains why it is structural rather than an accident of this file.

The one near-miss is [R004](./t0-seed/open-questions.md#r004-whose-advice-do-you-act-on):
the roommate. It is the only episode-shaped material in the source — a named
counterpart, a specific thing said, a response — and it carries no date. Under
ADR 0009 as written ("an entry citing no dated episode is inadmissible") it fails.
Under a bar reading "an episode, dated where available" it passes. **#86 should
know that one word in ADR 0009 is the difference between an empty record and a
record with one entry in it**, on this material.

⚠️ **The honest caveat, and it cuts the finding down.** A file cannot be pressed.
ADR 0009's bar assumes a BEI-shaped interview that *asks* for the incident, and
every inadmissibility above is "the file does not carry this," never "the founder
could not supply it." A live Jian-Yang would likely date the roommate exchange and
might produce episodes for two or three of the others. **This finding is evidence
about the bar's severity, not a measurement of the yield.** What it does establish
is the shape of the failure — see next.

## 2. The failure is not "too few episodes." It is that **dispositions and episodes are elicited by opposite questions**, and the source has only one kind.

Every candidate arrived as a *standing rule* — "I do not take investor", "contempt
for process you did not invent", "one food correct beat ten thousand food wrong."
Not one arrived as an occasion. That is not a defect in `jian-yang.md`; it is what
happens when you ask someone what they think.

The bar demands the other kind of material, and the other kind comes from the
other question. McClelland is explicit that the BEI works *because* it refuses the
general self-description. So the interview has to run both, and **the join is not
free**: the founder states a rule, the interview presses for an incident, and the
incident that comes back is frequently not an instance of the rule he stated. In
this cut, R002's disposition is *"no co-founder, no investor, no employee — any of
the three"* and the episode that would close it
([P101](./t1-accrual/positions/p101-obligation-outliving-runway.md)) reaches
**only equity**. The entry that survives is narrower than the thing he said, and
the difference is silently discarded.

**That narrowing is the artifact's whole value proposition and its whole cost, in
one place.** The gap between the rule he asserts and the rule his conduct
supports is precisely the espoused/theory-in-use gap #82 said was out of reach —
and the bar surfaces it. It also means a founder will consistently see the record
say less than he said, which is the adoption risk Fagerlin & Schneider's first
condition names.

## 3. The source contains an explicit never-list. **Not one item on it is admissible.**

`jian-yang.md` has a section headed **"Decisions already made, not up for
discussion"** — protected-value language, unprompted, in the source's own words.
Six items: the name, phone-only, one button, hotdog first, no cloud, no login.

**All six name product artifacts. All six fail ADR 0009 outright.** They are
vision and pitch fields wearing a never-list's clothes.

This is the sharpest confirmation of ADR 0009 the prototype produced, and it
arrives from an uncomfortable direction: the material a founder *volunteers* as
non-negotiable is the material most likely to be product decisions, because those
are the decisions he has actually made. The genuinely protected value in this file
(R002 — refusing the three) is not on his never-list at all; it is stated in
passing, in a paragraph about who he is.

**Consequence for the interview:** asking "what would you never do?" harvests the
wrong layer with high yield and high confidence. The prompt that worked here was
the reverse — read the volunteered never-list, discard it, and look at what the
person did not think to nominate.

## 4. The never-list, written both ways — and the two are not the same claim

#85 asked for this explicitly. Taking R002:

> **(a) What this founder said he would never trade.** "I will not take a
> co-founder, an investor, or an employee. Any of the three." Stated 2026-08-18 as
> a standing absolute, no amount named, no occasion cited.
>
> **(b) What this founder did not trade.** On 2026-11-03 he declined $150k for 8%,
> giving as his reason that the money bought eighteen months and the investor
> bought a claim on year four. On 2027-01-12 he paid a contractor for three weeks.

**(a) is unusable and (b) is usable, and the reason is not vividness — it is that
they answer different questions.** A reader holding "should we bring in a
part-time advisor for equity?" gets from (a) a confident *no* that is wrong at
least once (he hired), and from (b) a reasoning they can apply and a visible edge
where it stops.

(a) is also the version that reads *better*. It is shorter, more quotable, and
sounds more like him. #86 should expect the pull toward (a) to be constant and to
come from the founder himself.

## 5. Three components had **nothing to put in them**; one had too much and it was the wrong thing

- **Protected values** — one candidate (R002), with three of Baron & Spranca's
  five markers present. The instrument transfers; the yield is one.
- **Objectives (fundamental)** — **empty.** Everything in the file that looks like
  an objective is a means objective, which ADR 0009 excludes. There is no prompt
  list in a file, and Bond/Carlson/Keeney say free generation recovers about half;
  from a file it recovered none.
- **Guiding principles** — three candidates (R001, R003, R005), and this is the
  component doing most of the work in the cut. It is also, per #82, the one with
  the weakest evidential backing: one practitioner source, no instrument. **The
  component carrying the most weight has the least support behind it.**
- **Decision episodes** — **one, undated** (R004). The evidence layer that every
  other entry is required to cite is the emptiest layer in the source.

The shape is bad in a specific way: **the layer everything depends on is the layer
that is missing.** Not a thin record — an unsupported one.

## 6. ADR 0010's register is **two mechanisms**, and only one of them delivers what the ADR claims

ADR 0010 calls the register "an episode capture channel, not merely the next
sitting's agenda", on the reasoning that a register entry "is written at decision
time." That is true of *some* register entries and false of others, and the
prototype separates them:

| | Seed-opened (R001–R005) | Agent-appended at a no-match (R006, R007) |
|---|---|---|
| Written | at the sitting | **at the decision** |
| Records | a question that went nowhere | a decision that was taken anyway |
| Supplies a dated episode | **no** — the date must be recalled months later | **yes** — contemporaneously |
| Converting it is | self-report reconstruction | reading back a record |

Compare [P101](./t1-accrual/positions/p101-obligation-outliving-runway.md)
(recalled) against [P102](./t1-accrual/positions/p102-hands-not-judgement.md)
(contemporaneous). They pass the same bar and are not equally good evidence.
P101's date is exactly the hindsight-reconstructed material Parrish's decision
journal exists to avoid, and it clears the bar because the bar checks for *a date*,
not for *how the date was known*.

**This lands directly on [#108](https://github.com/98kb/skills/issues/108) item 20**
(register vs. agent-flagged backlog — one mechanism or two) and it changes the
argument. #108 frames the split as record-versus-backlog and warns against
merging mutable workflow state into frozen record. The prototype adds a second,
independent reason to split them: **they carry different evidential weight**, so
an entry converted at a later sitting needs to say which kind it was. That is a
schema field, not a workflow preference — and it survives whichever way #108
decides the workflow question.

**And it partially undercuts ADR 0010's mitigation argument.** The ADR credits
accretion with mitigating preference instability because it "converts a stated
future-directed preference into a time series of what the subject actually did."
That holds for agent-appended entries. It does not hold for seed-opened entries
closed on recall, which are still stated preference with a date attached.

## 7. The 200-line index budget is **not the binding constraint** — it is roughly 25 years away

Measured on this cut:

| | Routing lines | Index size | Budget used |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 (seed) | 0 | ~0.6KB | 0% |
| T1 (after one accrual) | 2 | ~2.6KB | ~3% of lines, ~10% of bytes |

At two positions per six-month sitting, 200 lines arrives in **~50 sittings ≈ 25
years**. Even at ten positions a sitting it is a decade.

Two consequences:

- **#108 item 9 can be settled cheaply.** The concern there is adopting 200 lines
  as though it were a measured cliff when #84 is explicit it is vendor-convergent
  practice. On this evidence the number's provenance barely matters, because the
  artifact does not approach it. Adopt it as a tripwire and stop arguing about it.
- **The real budget pressure is bytes, not lines, and it comes from the coverage
  statement.** At T1 the coverage statement is longer than the routing table,
  because there are more open questions than positions. On a record whose register
  outgrows its positions — which is this record, and §1 suggests it is the normal
  case early — **the always-loaded tier is dominated by a list of what the record
  does not cover.** Nobody designed for that. Whether the coverage statement even
  belongs in the always-loaded tier is a live question and is not currently
  anyone's ticket.

## 8. Freezing the record does **not** freeze what the record says, and this is not currently visible anywhere

The strongest unanticipated finding, and the one a static re-cut could not have
produced.

At T1 the routing line for P101 was drafted broadly and had to be **narrowed**
before publication, because P102 arrived at the same sitting and the broad wording
swallowed P102's queries. Retrieval is lexical, so two adjacent positions compete
for the same words and the broader line wins.

Per ISAD(G)/DACS the index is *description* and may be revised — so this is legal.
But note what it means:

**The same frozen entry, unedited, returns for a different set of questions before
and after the revision.** A reader on 2027-02-14 and a reader on 2027-02-16 get
different behaviour out of an identical record, and **nothing in the position file
records that anything changed.**

Three things follow that the map has not specified:

1. **The index line cannot live in the position file.** The entry is frozen and
   the line is revisable; putting a mutable field inside a frozen record is the
   category error #108 item 21 names in a different context. Authoring the index
   line is therefore a **separate act with separate provenance** — which sharpens
   #84's finding that index-line wording is a component rather than formatting.
2. **The index needs a description-control block** — ISAD(G) I.11's, verbatim:
   who described, when, revision number, what changed. The prototype's
   [T1 index](./t1-accrual/stance-index.md) carries one and it is doing real work.
3. **Adding a position can silently degrade the retrievability of an existing
   one.** This is the growth cost #85 was sent to find, and it is not a size
   problem — it is a *collision* problem, and it bites at 2 positions, not at 200.

## 9. The derived entry is correct, well-provenanced, and inert

Exercised in full at [D001](./t1-accrual/derived/d001-grants.md). The device came
apart into two halves that behave differently:

- **The bar works.** The scope cap stopped P101 being used on a grant question,
  twice, and no derived entry was ever mistaken for a position. The
  provenance-and-scope audit passes at read time, which is #83's surviving check
  doing its job.
- **The entry does nothing.** With no index line there is no path to it. A second
  session hit the same question, re-derived the same conclusion in-context, and
  never saw D001.

ADR 0010 flagged that this "may leave derived entries near-useless" and handed the
reconciliation to #108. The prototype confirms it and sharpens the question:
**a derived entry is not a retrieval device.** If it earns its place it earns it as
an *audit trail* — a record that an extrapolation was made, by whom, from what —
which is a different job with a different home. #108 should decide whether that
job is wanted, not whether the retrieval rule should be relaxed.

## 10. Does the five-donor assembly cohere, or is it stitched? — **Coherent, with one visible seam**

#87 found no established category holds more than four of the five required
properties, and #85 asked whether assembling one from five donors produces
something coherent. Writing it out: **mostly coherent.** IBIS's question-indexing,
POMR's register and defensibility cap, ISAD(G)'s record/description split, PROV-O's
derivation and the decision journal's dated append composed without fighting each
other — each occupies a different slot and none contradicts another. That is a
real positive result and it should be recorded as one.

**The seam is between POMR's register and ISAD(G)'s frozen record**, and it is §6:
POMR's problem list is a *live clinical working document* whose entries are opened
expecting to be closed, while ISAD(G)'s record is closed by construction. The
register inherits both parents and the prototype shows the strain — a seed-opened
entry behaves like ISAD(G) (a fact about the record, legitimately open forever)
and an agent-appended entry behaves like POMR (a thing wanting to be cleared).
**#108 item 20 is that seam**, and the prototype's contribution is that it is a
seam in the *donor set*, not merely a workflow ambiguity — which is why it cannot
be settled on convenience grounds.

## What this hands #86

The go/no-go inherits three things it did not have:

1. **The bar is more expensive than ADR 0009 priced it.** ADR 0009 said the choice
   is between a small defensible artifact and a large unfalsifiable one. On this
   material the small artifact is **empty at the seed**, and the record only exists
   from accrual onward. That converts ADR 0010's closing question — *is a record
   that is thin at ideation and thickens only if the founder comes back worth
   building?* — into a sharper one: **is a record that is empty at ideation and
   exists only if the founder comes back worth building?** #86 should answer that
   question, not the softer one.
2. **One word in ADR 0009 changes the output.** "A dated episode" versus "an
   episode" is 0 entries versus 1 on this cut. That is a decision, currently
   sitting unexamined inside a merged ADR.
3. **Three findings that survive a no-go** and belong to #108 or to Phase 2
   regardless: the index-revision problem (§8), the register's two origins (§6),
   and the derived entry's inertness (§9). If #86 calls no-go, §8 in particular is
   a general result about two-tier context artifacts and is worth keeping.

**What this prototype does not tell you.** It cannot tell you the yield of a real
sitting (§1's caveat), and the T1 accrual is constructed, so §§6–9 are evidence
about the *mechanism* and not about Jian-Yang. §§1–5 and §10 are evidence about
the material.
